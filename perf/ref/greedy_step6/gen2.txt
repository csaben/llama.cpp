The history of the bicycle begins in the early nineteenth century, when the first wheeled vehicles were created. These early bicycles were simple, two-wheeled contraptions that were propelled by the rider’s feet. They were not very efficient, and were often difficult to ride. However, they were a significant step forward in the development of transportation.

In the 1860s, the first practical bicycle was invented by a Frenchman named Pierre Michaux. This bicycle had a chain drive and a pedal system, which made it much easier to ride. It was also much faster than the earlier bicycles. The bicycle quickly became popular, and by the end of the century, it was a common sight on the streets of Europe and America.
