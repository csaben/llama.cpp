The history of the bicycle begins in the early nineteenth century, when men began to come up with ways to propel themselves by pedal and sprocket, and wealthy and interesting women dabbled in the mechanics of it. (I’ve lost my article on the notorious Lady Florence Spenser, who found the first way to maintain balance while pedaling and would presumably beat your father-in-law in a bicycling race, but given that today women are just as likely—perhaps more likely—to have a subscription to Bicycle Magazine as men, I have to suppose the progress is real and not merely aesthetic.) The early cyclists let their pedals drop the connection to the rear axle in order to navigate complicated driveways; they found ways of quickly hobbling their partners and themselves; they traded cloaks and skirts for sporty warm-ups, and their former wearers often threw a tantrum.
