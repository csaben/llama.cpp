The history of the bicycle begins in the early nineteenth century, when the idea was first conceptualised through the invention of a new sport. The inventors of these early wheels, William Farrer and his brother Trever, envisioned their creation as a device for playing a rather provocative sport called rifle-bouncing. The initial goal was to propel a ball about a court using the backswing of a tennis racket. A more ambitious quest followed, one which would endeavour to surpass all records for speed and feats of skill, and substitute a tiny, compact overcurrent sausage in its place.
