The history of the bicycle begins in the early nineteenth century, when wheeled toys were constructed for children in Germany and France. However, modern cycling originated in Sweden, where men and boys first pedaled similar wheeled toys equipped with chains in the early 1860s. In the early 1870s, solid-front-wheel “Ordinary bicycles” debuted in France and Belgium and increased rapidly in popularity. This invention made cycling “bourgeoise” and available to women and other middle-class people. In the 1920s, even trucks and automobiles were often accompanied by bicycles. Today, bicycles are constructed in countries all around the world for urban cyclists, off-road racers, and serious enthusiasts.
