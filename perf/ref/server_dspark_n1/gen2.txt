The history of the bicycle begins in the early nineteenth century, when the first truly wheeled transports came to life and the metaphor of the cycle gradually became deeply ingrained in Western culture. Like moonbeams, they weave through our experiences, occasionally catching us off guard at their ubiquity and issuing an invitation to ride

Thus did Mikey Bartlett put it in the opening zine of his Inside the Machine series, published between 1996 and 2001. The invitation is to consider the bicycle as a way of life, at once necessary and optional (if you’re not a regular cyclist, ask anyone who is for a definition of “necessary”). And to reflect on the many other artefacts and apparatuses that were created in its wake, still with us today, most of which are frequently questioned and argued over in the mainstream: helmets, panniers, punctures, Veripads, skid plates… the very language of “cycling”.
