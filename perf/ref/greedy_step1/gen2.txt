The history of the bicycle begins in the early nineteenth century, when the first wheeled vehicles were created. These early bicycles were simple, two-wheeled machines that were propelled by the rider’s feet. They were known as “draisines” or “velocipedes” and were popular among the wealthy and leisure class.

In the late 1800s, the bicycle industry began to grow rapidly. The first mass-produced bicycles were created in the United States, and they quickly became popular among the working class. The bicycle was seen as a practical and affordable mode of transportation, and it was used by people of all ages and backgrounds.

The bicycle industry continued to evolve throughout the twentieth century. In the 1920s, the first safety bicycle was created, which featured a chain drive and a rear wheel that was larger than the front wheel. This design made the bicycle more stable and easier to ride.
