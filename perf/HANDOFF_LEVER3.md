## Round 2 in progress

The starting tree is 2b01a78. The baseline libraries from cdef6ff are in build/p7l3/base-libs. The fresh four-depth benchmark uses those libraries and the common GPU0 environment above. The floor probe captures the 193 live matrix descriptors and snapshots each routed selection immediately before consumption during the first single-token step after the 200-token corpus prefix. The timing comparison will use the same weight addresses and Q8_1 inputs in both schedules.

The first probe measured 2,006,498,304 bytes over 193 matvecs. All 2,494,400 output rows passed the stock-dot comparison. At 16 rows/tile and 128 threads, the resident dot took 2.69 ms, the stock 193-node graph 2.87-2.90 ms, and the matched 193-node tiled graph 3.53 ms. The corrected volatile read control took 2.34 ms. This triggers the real dense-chain branch. [Raw times](p7l3r2/floor-first.txt), [captured routes](p7l3r2/routes.txt)

The first real dense visit fuses both IQ4_NL matrices, the Q8 attention scan, the short convolution and its state writes. A per-KV-head instruction list releases attention from the last input-tile producer. All 48 visits ran in one cooperative launch each and preserved the 64-row reference logit hash. The first trace cost 37.78 us/visit versus 31.79 us for the stock graph. The two-stage control was slower. The measured kernels used 128 or 168 registers/thread. The next screen varies scheduling, register budget and output-weight staging. [Visit trace](p7l3r2/visit-first.txt), [floor launch sweep](p7l3r2/floor-sweep.txt)

The late-gate schedule reaches the first visit-level win. It streams the 5,528/5,592 scan-input rows first, overlaps the remaining 3,072 gate rows with attention, and applies the gate when the scan ends. A single cooperative kernel ran in 31.63 us vs 31.84 us for the same-build graph. A three-launch schedule took 31.32 us at 24 gate CTAs/head. Both matched the reference logit file. A deeper task queue with shared-memory cp.async staging and 2-4 CTAs/SM was slower. The next experiment extends across the output convolution into the real expert router. [Traces](p7l3r2/gate-traces.txt), [queue](p7l3r2/queue-screen.txt), [gate](p7l3r2/gate-screen.txt), [three-launch screen](p7l3r2/stage-screen.txt)

The extension through routing ran at 24 visits/token and preserved the full reference logits. Its projection workers and 16 preloading router CTAs shared a cooperative grid. It lost the paired tg128 runs to the dense schedule alone. The upstream mix experiment also covers the true residual mix, normalization and packing while workers use cp.async on up to 56 rows each. The follow-up tag includes both extensions and the separate gate-stream and long-scan variants. [Router comparison](p7l3r2/router-trace.txt), [paired tg128](p7l3r2/router-screen.txt), [mix sweep](p7l3r2/mix-screen.txt)

Splitting the long-cache block into eight attention warps and four parallel gate warps gave 217.22/198.83/187.20/173.19 tok/s in the first four-depth sweep. The 8+8 configuration lost. A second short-context layout packs the same IQ4_NL codes before their FP16 scales per row and joins QKV with WO at load time. Both packed schedules match the reference logit hash. The next experiment folds the eight-expert reduction and recurrent update directly into the down rows. [Warp screen](p7l3r2/warps.txt), [layout screen](p7l3r2/pack-screen.txt)

The layout-enabled floor probe checked three distinct, live 48-visit routing traces at corpus positions 200, 212 and 248. Native and lossless dense-packed layouts both stayed below 2.81 ms in this build. The expert experiment permutes unchanged Q2_0 bits so each dp4a input needs two fewer byte-permute instructions. At all 44 eligible visits, a second kernel joins all eight expert slots per output tile and applies the real FFN convolution/state update. The traced 4-row native-weight fusion took 12.70 us versus 12.17 us for the graph pair. The real mix extension ran at 24 of 48 visits. The selected implementation will keep just the gate overlap and reuse the graph allocator and the existing output projection. [Three-route audit](p7l3r2/layout-floor.txt)

The selected candidate keeps the Q8 activation live through attention with the existing CUDA allocator callback and reuses the native output projection/conv. No repacked model or extra activation copy is required. All 48 visits appeared in Nsight. The interval fell from 31.803 to 30.143 us/visit in the same build, and the first 64 full-vocabulary rows were bit-identical. Use IK_DENSE_VISIT=1 to enable it. A clean archival build of 16264c6 and long-context validation are in build/p7l3r2. [Off](p7l3r2/selected-trace-0.txt), [on](p7l3r2/selected-trace-1.txt)

The exact 16264c6 archival build and the selected build agree on both requested PPL workloads. All 64 vocabulary rows after 200 tokens, and eight rows each after 4096, 16384 and 32768 tokens, matched byte for byte. The controlled four-depth benchmarks start at or below 63 C on GPU0. The supplemental prototype also puts the entire dense chain and the next direct router in the same cooperative launch. [Logits](p7l3r2/logits.txt), [PPL](p7l3r2/ppl.txt)

A bitwise floor directly measured 820 CTAs on 82 distinct SMs and matched all 2,494,400 production FP32 outputs. It took 2.775 ms against 2.935/2.948 ms for stock. The complete dense-plus-router resident kernel is also exact, including its top-8 selections as consumed by the following experts. At the 24 direct visits it took 49.23-49.62 us against 40.80 us for the dense kernel plus stock router. The corrected native/packed expert epilogues also reproduce the reference. [Bitwise floor](p7l3r2/bitwise-floor.txt), [router coverage and timing](p7l3r2/resident-router-trace.txt), [prototype hashes](p7l3r2/extended-hashes.txt)

Both returned identical logits. The same-build, per-depth off/on/on/off runs and the DSpark/operator suite complete the handoff.
